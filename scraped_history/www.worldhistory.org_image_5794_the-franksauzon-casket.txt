This spectacular whalebone casket was probably made in the Anglo-
Kingdom of Northumbria
. Modelled on early Christian caskets, it was most likely created in a monastery for a significant (perhaps a royal) patron. The casket's imagery, accompanied by runic and Latin inscriptions, come from an array of sources, including Germanic and
, and historical events. Made in a time where
had not long been established in Anglo-Saxon
, its carvings reflect a wish to convey Christian messages by relating them to the remembered pagan past. Donated by Sir
. Early 700s. From Auzon, Haute-Loire, Auvergne, modern-day France. (The British Museum, London).
