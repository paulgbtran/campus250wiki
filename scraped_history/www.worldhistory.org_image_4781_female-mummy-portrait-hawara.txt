Mummy portrait of the head and shoulders of a dark-haired young woman from Hawara cemetery,
, early 2nd century CE.
, London (with thanks to The Petrie Museum of Egyptian Archaeology, UCL).
The Petrie Museum has the largest collection of these "portraits" outside of Egypt. Originally, these portraits were placed over the mummified body; these images were hailed as the first lifelike representations of real people on their first exhibition in London in 1888 CE. The portraits were excavated in 1888-1889 and 1910-1911. This woman wears a brown robe,
earrings, and simple necklace. "Isarous" is written in
across the background. An analysis of the plate revealed the presence of beeswax as the binding medium.
