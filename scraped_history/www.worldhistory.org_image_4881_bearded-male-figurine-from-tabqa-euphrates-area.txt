During the late 3rd millennium BCE, the area of the middle Euphrates developed a distinctive regional
. Between 1963-1973 CE, an international rescue mission excavated a large number of sites in this region which were threatened by flooding as a result of the construction of the Tabqa dam. One such site was Tell es-Sweyhat. Excavations by T. A. Hollad on behalf of the British School of
revealed extensive early and middle
occupation. Figurines with pillar bases, elaborate hairstyle and applied eyes and "scarves" are characteristic of the Tabqa-Euphraets area. From Tabqa, Ar-Raqqah, modern-day northern
Age, 2400-2000 BCE. (The British Museum, London)
