* **Origins section**: Changed "Orange Street Friends Meeting House" to "Arch Street Friends Meeting House". The historic meeting house in Philadelphia famous for representing Quaker plain dress and community identity is the Arch Street Friends Meeting House (built 1803–1811).
* **Development section**: Corrected the description of E.W. Clay's satirical prints. Clay's famous 1828–1830 series *Life in Philadelphia* primarily caricatured free African Americans and middle-class social aspirations, rather than depicting upper-class Quakers ignoring Irish immigrants.
* **Development section**: Corrected the claim that Quaker characters are absent until the final scene where a nameless Quaker departs on a boat. In George Lippard's novel *The Quaker City*, Quaker characters are entirely absent from the plot; Lippard used the title ironically to contrast the city's Quaker reputation with its dark urban realities.
