* **Removed claim that Gloria Dei is the "second-oldest church building in continuous use in the United States"** (Infobox & Lead): While Gloria Dei (1700) is indeed the oldest church building in Pennsylvania, several older church buildings exist and remain in active use in the United States, including Holy Trinity Church (Old Swedes) in Wilmington, DE (1699), San Miguel Chapel in Santa Fe, NM (c. 1610), and St. Luke's Church in Benns Church, VA (17th century).
* **Corrected description of 1703 tower/steeple construction** (Infobox, History timeline, History narrative, Architecture, ASCII diagram, and Table): Clarified that the western entry porch and lower tower added in 1703 were built of brick matching the Flemish bond nave (built to buttress the spreading nave walls), topped with a timber belfry/spire, rather than being an entirely timber-framed/wooden structure.
* **Removed incorrect assertion regarding mid-19th century stained glass** (Architecture narrative & summary table): Removed the fabricated detail stating that Gloria Dei has a "sole stained glass window" above the pulpit that is the "earliest surviving example of American-made stained glass" in the region. Gloria Dei features leaded clear glass windows. Replaced this text with details on authentic interior artifacts, such as the 1640s carved wooden cherubim from Printz Hall/Tinicum Island and the historic Swedish baptismal font.
