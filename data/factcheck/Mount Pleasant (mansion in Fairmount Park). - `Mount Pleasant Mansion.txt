* **Section Headings**: Renamed biographical section headings (`3. Early life and education`, `4. Career`, and `5. Personal life`) to appropriate architectural and historical section titles (`3. Design and construction`, `4. History and ownership`, and `5. Interiors and domestic workforce`).
* **Building Construction Materials**: Corrected descriptions of Mount Pleasant's masonry in Sections 2 and 3. The mansion is not a brick building constructed with Flemish bond brickwork; it is constructed of rubble fieldstone coated in yellow stucco scored to resemble cut ashlar stone blocks, framed by red brick trim around window and door openings.
* **Ownership Sequence**: Corrected the post-Revolutionary War ownership history in Section 4. Chief Justice Edward Shippen never purchased Mount Pleasant in 1792. Following state confiscation, the estate was bought at auction in 1784 by merchant Blair McClenachan, who subsequently sold it to Jonathan Williams in 1792.
* **Baron von Steuben's Encampment**: Corrected the details regarding Baron von Steuben's occupancy in Section 4. Von Steuben leased Mount Pleasant in 1781–1782 as a private countryside retreat, not as a headquarters for reorganizing the Continental Army (which took place during the Valley Forge encampment in 1777–1778).
