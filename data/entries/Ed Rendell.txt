## Lead section

**Edward Graham Rendell** (born January 5, 1944) is an American lawyer, politician, and author who served as the 45th Governor of Pennsylvania from 2003 to 2011 and as the 96th Mayor of Philadelphia from 1992 to 2000. A prominent figure in local, state, and national Democratic politics, Rendell also served as the District Attorney of Philadelphia from 1978 to 1986 and General Chair of the Democratic National Committee from 1999 to 2001.

Rendell first achieved national recognition during his tenure as Mayor of Philadelphia in the 1990s. Taking office during a period of impending municipal bankruptcy and severe fiscal deficits, Rendell enacted rigorous budget cuts, restructured labor agreements, and spearheaded downtown revitalization initiatives. His energetic governance and economic restructuring of Center City earned him praise as a model for modern urban administration, revitalizing Philadelphia's commercial center and civic morale.

As Governor of Pennsylvania, Rendell expanded state investment in public education, early learning programs, and physical infrastructure. His administration successfully championed the legalization of slot machine gaming to generate property tax relief for residents, directed state funding toward clean energy technology, and navigated Pennsylvania through the economic strains of the 2008 global financial crisis. 

Following his tenure in elective office, Rendell remained an influential public commentator on infrastructure investment, urban planning, and national policy. He authored political memoirs, taught at the University of Pennsylvania, and served on various public service boards and bipartisan advocacy coalitions.

## Early life and education

Edward Graham Rendell was born in New York City on January 5, 1944, to Jesse T. Rendell and Emma Levitt. His parents were of Jewish descent and resided in New York, where his father worked in the textile garment industry. Rendell attended the Riverdale Country School before moving to Philadelphia to pursue higher education at the University of Pennsylvania. He graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree in 1965.

After completing his undergraduate studies, Rendell enrolled at the Villanova University School of Law, earning his Juris Doctor degree in 1968. That same year, Rendell enlisted in the United States Army Reserve, serving as a lieutenant until his honorable discharge in 1974. Following his admission to the Pennsylvania bar, Rendell chose to remain in Philadelphia, beginning his career in public service within the city's legal system.

## Career

### District Attorney of Philadelphia (1978–1986)

Rendell joined the Philadelphia District Attorney’s Office in 1968, working his way up through the prosecutor ranks to become Chief of the Homicide Division. In 1977, at age 33, Rendell challenged incumbent District Attorney F. Emmett Fitzpatrick in the Democratic primary, campaigning on a platform of anti-corruption and legal reform. Rendell won the primary and the subsequent general election, becoming the youngest District Attorney in Philadelphia's history upon taking office in January 1978.

As District Attorney, Rendell emphasized high-conviction prosecution for violent offenders while expanding victim support services. He was re-elected to a second term in 1981. Rendell sought the Democratic nomination for Governor of Pennsylvania in 1986 but lost in the primary to Robert P. Casey. In 1987, Rendell challenged incumbent Mayor W. Wilson Goode in the Democratic primary for Mayor of Philadelphia, suffering a narrow defeat in a racially divided contest.

### Mayor of Philadelphia (1992–2000)

In 1991, Rendell ran once more for Mayor of Philadelphia and won the general election, taking office in January 1992. Upon assuming office, Rendell inherited a projected $250 million fiscal deficit—the largest in the city's history—along with a credit rating reduced to junk bond status. Philadelphia was near financial collapse, facing massive operational shortfalls and declining tax revenues due to suburban flight and industrial decline.

Rendell executed an aggressive fiscal turnaround strategy. He negotiated concessions with municipal labor unions, privatized selected municipal services, reduced healthcare expenditures, and instituted strict administrative accountability. Through these measures, Rendell eliminated the city's deficit within his first term and generated financial surpluses.

Beyond fiscal stabilization, Rendell focused on urban redevelopment:
* **Avenue of the Arts:** He promoted the transformation of South Broad Street into a cultural district anchored by the Kimmel Center for the Performing Arts.
* **Tourism and Hospitality:** Rendell supported the expansion of the Pennsylvania Convention Center and promoted tourism initiatives to reposition Center City as a regional entertainment and dining hub.
* **Historic and Regional Development:** He advocated for the redevelopment of the Philadelphia Naval Shipyard into a commercial business park following its closure by the federal government.

Rendell was re-elected in a landslide in 1995. His success in restoring economic viability to a large American city earned him wide national acclaim. President Bill Clinton praised Rendell's approach, and Rendell served as General Chair of the Democratic National Committee during the 2000 presidential campaign.

### Governor of Pennsylvania (2003–2011)

In 2002, Rendell secured the Democratic nomination for Governor of Pennsylvania, defeating Auditor General Bob Casey Jr. in a competitive primary. In the general election, Rendell defeated Republican nominee Mike Fisher, taking office as Pennsylvania's 45th Governor in January 2003. He was re-elected in 2006, defeating Republican challenger and former professional football player Lynn Swann.

During his two gubernatorial terms, Rendell enacted significant legislative and policy changes:
* **Education Policy:** Rendell increased state financial contributions to public K-12 education, introduced state-funded Pre-K Counts, and supported full-day kindergarten programs across school districts.
* **Legalization of Gaming:** In 2004, Rendell signed Act 71, legalizing slot machine gaming at licensed racetracks and commercial casinos across the state, dedicating gaming tax revenue toward property tax relief for Pennsylvania homeowners.
* **Alternative Energy and Environment:** In 2004, Rendell signed the Alternative Energy Portfolio Standards Act, requiring state energy suppliers to increase reliance on renewable sources.
* **Infrastructure Reinvestment:** Rendell oversaw substantial state bond programs designed to modernize Pennsylvania's aging bridges, transit systems, water infrastructure, and highways.

Rendell's second term coincided with the 2008 national financial crisis. He worked with federal officials to deploy American Recovery and Reinvestment Act stimulus funds toward state education, job retention, and construction programs.

## Personal life

In 1971, Rendell married Marjorie O. Rendell (née Orenstein), who served as a judge on the United States District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania and later on the United States Court of Appeals for the Third Circuit. The couple has one son, Jesse Rendell. In 2011, after Rendell completed his tenure as governor, the couple announced an amicable separation, though they remained legally married.

Rendell is widely known for his candid, informal public demeanor and keen interest in sports. For several years following his governorship, Rendell served as a post-game analyst for the Philadelphia Eagles on regional television broadcasts.

In May 2018, Rendell announced publicly that he had been diagnosed with Parkinson's disease. Following his announcement, he became an advocate for neurological disease research, encouraging public awareness and medical support for individuals living with movement disorders.

---

## Legacy

Ed Rendell’s legacy is tied to the municipal survival and urban transformation of Philadelphia in the late 20th century. Urban historians credit his administration with stemming the city's fiscal emergency and providing a blueprint for post-industrial downtown revitalization that converted Center City into a residential, cultural, and commercial district.

At the state level, Rendell’s tenure as governor reoriented state spending priorities toward public education funding equity and infrastructure development. His passage of expanded gaming legislation fundamentally reshaped state revenue streams and commercial gaming across the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania.

In 2012, Rendell published his political memoir, *A Nation of Wusses: How America's Leaders Lost the Guts to Make Us Great*, offering commentary on national legislative gridlock and advocating for bold civic leadership. He also co-founded Building America’s Future Educational Fund, a bipartisan coalition dedicated to promoting federal and state infrastructure investments.
