### Lead section

**Evelyn Taylor Price** (often referred to historically as **Evelyn Taylor**, **Mrs. Evelyn Taylor Price**, or **Mrs. Eli Kirk Price II**) was an influential twentieth-century American civic leader, community organizer, and philanthropist based in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. She served as a pivotal figure in Center City urban preservation, horticultural advocacy, and community welfare during the mid-twentieth century. Most notably, Price served as the long-term president of the Flower Market Association and was actively involved with the Rittenhouse Square Improvement Association, two major civic organizations that shaped the physical landscape and social tradition of Rittenhouse Square.

Through her administrative stewardship and community organizing, Price played a central role in maintaining Rittenhouse Square as one of Philadelphia’s premier public parks and cultural gathering spaces. Under her guidance, the annual Rittenhouse Square Flower Market developed into a cornerstone civic tradition, generating funds for regional health and social welfare programs while promoting public horticulture and neighborhood pride in Center City Philadelphia.

Following her passing, Price was honored by her civic peers through the commissioning of a permanent public art monument in Rittenhouse Square. In 1947, the Flower Market Association commissioned acclaimed Philadelphia sculptor Beatrice Fenton to create the *Evelyn Taylor Price Memorial Sundial*. Located along Walnut Street between 18th and 19th Streets, the bronze-and-granite sculpture features a whimsical sunflower motif, directly referencing Price’s dedication to floral markets, urban greenery, and park beautification.

Today, Price’s historical legacy remains visible in Center City Philadelphia. The *Evelyn Taylor Price Memorial Sundial* is owned and maintained by the City of Philadelphia. Price's impact on urban spaces and civic life continues to be interpreted by public history institutions, including the Association for Public Art (aPA) through its *Museum Without Walls: AUDIO* initiative, which features reflections from municipal historians and her descendants, including her granddaughter Evelyn Price Scott.

### Early life and education

Evelyn Taylor Price was born and raised in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, into a family deeply embedded in the civic and cultural life of Greater Philadelphia. During the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, Philadelphia experienced rapid industrialization alongside a growing municipal reform movement. Prominent women across Center City increasingly established private associations, social welfare funds, and neighborhood improvement societies to address urban density and advocate for public parks and open spaces.

Growing up in an era when public parks were increasingly recognized as essential sanctuaries for public health and urban recreation, Price developed an early interest in horticulture, public aesthetics, and community service. Her early formal education and private upbringing reflected the social expectations of Philadelphia’s civic-minded upper class, emphasizing social responsibility, patronage of the arts, and neighborhood stewardship.

---

### Career

Price’s primary public career developed through her leadership in non-profit municipal organizations and civic improvement campaigns centered around Rittenhouse Square. As president of the Flower Market Association and an active civic advocate, Price directed efforts to preserve the historic integrity, security, and landscape architecture of the square. Established to protect the public park from suburban flight and commercial overdevelopment, neighborhood civic initiatives under Price's administration focused on maintaining green infrastructure, supporting public amenities, and organizing local residents around urban conservation.

In her responsibilities as president of the Flower Market Association, Price oversaw the Rittenhouse Square Flower Market, held annually in the park, which was one of Philadelphia's most celebrated civic events. Under Price's multi-year presidency, the market expanded its public outreach and fundraising capabilities. Vendors, florists, and volunteers transformed the perimeter of the square into a bustling marketplace, raising crucial funds for local children’s charities, hospitals, and urban greening projects.

Price’s work bridged municipal governance, urban horticulture, and non-profit fundraising. Her efforts ensured that Rittenhouse Square remained not only a well-maintained green space but also an active venue for cross-class community interaction, artistic display, and civic celebrations.

---

### Personal life

Price resided in Center City Philadelphia, maintaining close personal and professional ties with regional civic leaders, artists, and preservation advocates. Her home life was deeply integrated into the philanthropic networks of the neighborhood. 

Price's commitment to community stewardship was inherited by her family. Her granddaughter, Evelyn Price Scott, has preserved family oral histories and contributed to institutional archives documenting Price’s civic contributions and the creation of her memorial in Rittenhouse Square. Price also maintained relationships with regional artists, supporting Philadelphia sculptors and landscape artists who contributed to the city's public parks.

---

### Legacy

Price’s most visible enduring legacy is the *Evelyn Taylor Price Memorial Sundial*, commissioned shortly after her death by the Flower Market Association to honor her decades of service. Located along Walnut Street between 18th and 19th Streets in Rittenhouse Square, the memorial was executed in 1947 by renowned Philadelphia sculptor Beatrice Fenton (1887–1983), who was also known for her *Seaweed Girl Fountain* at the Fairmount Park Horticulture Center.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
|               EVELYN TAYLOR PRICE MEMORIAL SUNDIAL                |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Artist:          Beatrice Fenton (1887–1983)                      |
| Year:            1947                                             |
| Medium:          Bronze figure on a granite base                  |
| Dimensions:      Sculpture: 4'11" H x 2'6" W x 2'0" D             |
|                  Base: 3'0" H x 3'0" W x 2'0" D                   |
| Location:        Rittenhouse Square, Philadelphia, PA             |
| Ownership:       City of Philadelphia                             |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

The bronze sculpture measures 4 feet 11 inches in height, resting on a granite base measuring 3 feet in height. It depicts two cheerful, unclothed children raising the head of a large sunflower, which serves as a functional outdoor sundial. The composition plays artfully on the thematic connection between sunflowers and sunlight. Art historian Beatrice Gillman Proske characterized Fenton's work for Price as "a poetical reminder of the fleeting joys of sunshine."

The sculpture remains in Rittenhouse Square under the ownership of the City of Philadelphia. It is featured in the Association for Public Art’s *Museum Without Walls: AUDIO* program (Segment Producer: Alex Lewis). The audio guide includes archival recordings of sculptor Beatrice Fenton explaining her artistic vision, historical commentary by Dr. Page Talbott (former president of the Historical Society of Pennsylvania), and personal reflections from Price's granddaughter, Evelyn Price Scott.
