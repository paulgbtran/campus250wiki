## 2. Lead section

**Patti LaBelle** (born Patricia Louise Holte; May 24, 1944) is an American singer, actress, and cultural icon from Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Widely revered as the "Godmother of Soul," LaBelle is celebrated for her soaring soprano vocal range, dramatic stage presence, and multi-decade career spanning gospel, R&B, rock, pop, and soul music.

LaBelle began her career in the early 1960s as the lead vocalist of Patti LaBelle and the Bluebelles. In the 1970s, the group reinvented itself as the groundbreaking glam-rock/funk trio **Labelle**, achieving worldwide fame with their 1974 number-one hit "Lady Marmalade." Following the group's split in 1976, LaBelle launched a highly successful solo career, earning critical and commercial acclaim with signature songs such as "You Are My Friend," "If Only You Knew," "New Attitude," and "On My Own."

While emerging from the same mid-twentieth-century Philadelphia music ecosystem that birthed the "Sound of Philadelphia" (TSOP), LaBelle carved out her own distinct artistic path, recording primarily for labels such as Epic and MCA rather than being a roster signee of Philadelphia International Records (PIR). Nevertheless, she remains one of the city's most beloved cultural figures and musical ambassadors.

Recognized as a vital pillar of Philadelphia's artistic heritage, LaBelle has received numerous civic and national honors. In 2016, she was awarded the prestigious Marian Anderson Award in her hometown, and in 2019, Philadelphia formally dedicated a section of South Broad Street as "Patti LaBelle Way."

## 3. Early life and education

Patti LaBelle was born Patricia Louise Holte in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, on May 24, 1944. She grew up in Southwest Philadelphia during a period of significant demographic and cultural expansion, as African American migration transformed the city's urban neighborhoods into vibrant centers for religious, social, and artistic life.

During LaBelle's formative years in the 1950s, church choir traditions served as the bedrock for Black vocal music in Philadelphia. LaBelle began singing as a child at the Beulah Baptist Church, where her powerful vocal style was deeply influenced by gospel music traditions—marked by intense emotional expression, melismatic runs, and narrative urgency.

In 1959, LaBelle formed a high school vocal group called the Ordettes with Jean Rowlette, Yvonne Houltez, and Johnnie Dawson. By 1961, after lineup changes that brought in Cindy Birdsong, Nona Hendryx, and Sarah Dash, local record producer Bernard Montague took over management, and the group was renamed Patti LaBelle and the Bluebelles. Their early tracks were released on local Philadelphia labels such as Newtown Records and Cameo Parkway Records, providing a launching pad for their national recording career.

## 4. Career

### Early Success: Patti LaBelle and the Bluebelles
In 1962, Patti LaBelle and the Bluebelles achieved their first top-40 hit with "I Sold My Heart to the Junkman," released on Newtown Records (and distributed via Cameo-Parkway). Over the next several years, the group scored additional hits, including "Down the Aisle (Show Prayer)" and their popular rendition of "You'll Never Walk Alone." In 1967, Cindy Birdsong departed the group to join The Supremes, leaving LaBelle, Hendryx, and Dash to continue as a trio.

### The Labelle Era and "Lady Marmalade"
In 1970, under the direction of manager Vicki Wickham, the group restructured its image and sound, shortening its name to **Labelle**. Ditching traditional girl-group gowns for futuristic, glam-rock costumes and incorporating elements of hard rock, funk, and disco, Labelle became one of the most innovative acts of the 1970s. 

In 1974, Labelle released the album *Nightbirds* on Epic Records, recorded in New Orleans and produced by legendary songwriter and producer Allen Toussaint. The album produced the international smash hit "Lady Marmalade," which reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100. In 1975, Labelle made history as the first African American pop group to perform at the Metropolitan Opera House in New York City.

### Solo Career Success
Labelle disbanded in 1976 due to creative differences, after which Patti LaBelle launched a solo career. She released her self-titled debut solo album on Epic Records in 1977, which featured her classic ballad "You Are My Friend." 

In the 1980s, LaBelle achieved massive pop crossover success after signing with MCA Records. Her contributions to the 1984 *Beverly Hills Cop* soundtrack—including the upbeat hits "New Attitude" and "Stir It Up"—catapulted her into mainstream stardom. In 1986, she released her number-one album *Winner in You*, which featured the chart-topping pop and R&B duet "On My Own" with Michael McDonald.

Throughout the 1990s and 2000s, LaBelle continued to release acclaimed albums, winning a Grammy Award for Best Female R&B Vocal Performance for her 1991 album *Burnin'*.

## 5. Personal life

Patti LaBelle has maintained strong personal, cultural, and civic ties to Greater Philadelphia throughout her life. Raised within the city's African American community, her personal identity remains deeply tied to Philadelphia's neighborhoods and civic traditions.

She married Armstead Edwards in 1969; the couple had one son, Zuri Kye Edwards (who became her manager), and adopted two sons before divorcing amicably in 2001. Beyond her musical career, LaBelle is an accomplished author of cookbooks and the founder of "Patti's Good Life," a successful line of food products and baked goods. She is also a long-time advocate for diabetes awareness, HIV/AIDS relief, and cancer research.

---

## 6. Legacy

Patti LaBelle's legacy is defined by her dynamic vocal ability, emotional intensity, and pioneer status as both a group frontwoman and a solo powerhouse. Her influence spans generations of vocalists across R&B, soul, pop, and gospel.

Her contributions to music and her hometown have been commemorated through numerous major honors:
* **Marian Anderson Award**: In November 2016, LaBelle received the Marian Anderson Award in Philadelphia, named after the historic African American contralto and civil rights figure.
* **Patti LaBelle Way**: On July 2, 2019, the City of Philadelphia officially renamed a section of South Broad Street (between Spruce and Locust Streets) as "Patti LaBelle Way."
* **Hall of Fame Inductions**: She has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame (for "Lady Marmalade"), the Apollo Theater Hall of Fame, and received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.
