### Lead section

**Harry Norbert Kalas** (March 26, 1936 – April 13, 2009) was an American sportscaster and voiceover artist best known as the play-by-play voice of the Philadelphia Phillies in Major League Baseball (MLB) from 1971 until his death in 2009. Recognized for his resonant, deep baritone voice, melodious delivery, and signature home run call—*"That ball is outta here!"*—Kalas became one of the most beloved figures in Philadelphia sports history and a landmark personality in American sports broadcasting.

Over a career spanning nearly five decades, Kalas called many of the most historic moments in Philadelphia athletics, including the opening of Veterans Stadium in 1971, the Phillies' first World Series title in 1980, Mike Schmidt's 500th home run in 1987, and the team's second World Series championship in 2008. His long-standing broadcasting partnership with Hall of Fame center fielder Richie Ashburn ("Harry and Whitey") endured for 27 seasons and remains one of the most iconic duos in baseball radio history.

Beyond baseball, Kalas gained national fame as a chief narrator for NFL Films starting in 1975, succeeding the legendary John Facenda as the primary voice of the studio in 1984. His authoritative tone narrated decades of national football highlights, television specials, and commercials, establishing his voice as a staple of American popular culture.

In recognition of his distinguished career in baseball broadcasting, Kalas received the prestigious Ford C. Frick Award from the National Baseball Hall of Fame in 2002. He was inducted into the National Sportscasters and Sportswriters Association Hall of Fame in 2008. Following his sudden death in the press box at Nationals Park in 2009, Kalas was memorialized across the Delaware Valley, leaving a permanent legacy reflected in permanent honors at Citizens Bank Park and throughout the city of Philadelphia.

### Early life and education

Harry Norbert Kalas was born on March 26, 1936, in Naperville, Illinois, the son of a Methodist minister. Raised in the Chicago suburban area, Kalas developed a passion for sports and broadcasting at a young age while listening to radio play-by-play coverage of the Chicago Cubs and Chicago White Sox. He attended Naperville High School, where he participated in student athletics and expressed an early intent to pursue a career behind the microphone.

Following high school graduation, Kalas enrolled at the University of Iowa. During his collegiate years, he gained practical radio experience working at the university station, WSUI, where he covered campus athletics and refined his signature broadcast tone. He graduated from Iowa in 1959 with a Bachelor of Arts degree in Speech and Radio/Television/Film.

Shortly after graduating from college, Kalas was drafted into the United States Army. Stationed in Hawaii, he was assigned to radio duties and began announcing minor league baseball games for the Hawaii Islanders of the Pacific Coast League. His work with the Islanders served as his professional entry point into baseball play-by-play commentary, catching the attention of major league executives.

### Career

#### Early career and Houston Astros (1965–1970)
In 1965, Kalas was hired as part of the original broadcasting team for the Houston Astros, coinciding with the opening of the Houston Astrodome. Working alongside lead broadcaster Gene Elston, Kalas spent six seasons in Houston, establishing a reputation as a crisp, precise play-by-play announcer capable of seamlessly blending statistical depth with warm narrative storytelling.

#### Philadelphia Phillies (1971–2009)
In 1971, the Philadelphia Phillies hired Kalas to replace broadcaster Les Keiter as the team moved into Veterans Stadium. Kalas joined a broadcast booth that included former Phillies outfielder Richie Ashburn and veteran announcer By Saam. Kalas and Ashburn rapidly developed a rare, genuine rapport on air; their humorous banter, shared love of the game, and complementary styles turned "Harry and Whitey" into a regional cultural phenomenon across Pennsylvania, New Jersey, and Delaware.

Kalas called many monumental milestones during his 38-year tenure with the franchise:
* **1971:** The opening game of Veterans Stadium on April 10, 1971.
* **1980:** The Phillies' run to their first World Series title. (Due to MLB radio rules at the time prohibiting local announcers from calling World Series games on radio, Kalas was unable to make the radio call of the final out, prompting fan outcry that eventually led MLB to change its regulations).
* **1987:** Mike Schmidt's 500th career home run in Pittsburgh on April 18, 1987 (*"Swing and a long drive, there it is, number 500! The 500th home run of the career of Mike Schmidt!"*).
* **2004:** The inaugural game at Citizens Bank Park on April 12, 2004.
* **2008:** The Phillies' second World Series championship on October 29, 2008, where Kalas delivered his most famous championship call: *"One strike away. Lidge stretches. 0-2 pitch on the way. Swing and a miss! Struck him out! The Philadelphia Phillies are 2008 World Champions of baseball!"*

#### NFL Films and national voiceover work
In 1975, Kalas joined NFL Films as a narrator. Following the death of legendary voice John Facenda in 1984, Kalas became the primary narrator for NFL Films productions. His booming baritone voiced national highlight reels, Super Bowl retrospectives, and the popular weekly series *Inside the NFL*. Kalas also voiced high-profile corporate advertising campaigns for companies such as Campbell's Soup, Coors Light, and Chunky Soup.

---

### Personal life

Kalas married his wife, Eileen, in 1974. Together, they raised three sons: Todd, Brad, and Kane. The family resided in the Philadelphia metropolitan area for several decades. Kalas's eldest son, Todd Kalas, pursued a career in sports broadcasting, serving as a play-by-play announcer for Major League Baseball teams including the Tampa Bay Rays and Houston Astros.

Off the air, Kalas was renowned among colleagues, players, and fans for his warmth, modesty, and accessibility. He was an avid golfer, enjoyed fine cigars, and spent considerable personal time interacting with fans at community events across the Greater Philadelphia area.

---

### Legacy

On April 13, 2009, Kalas collapsed in the press box at Nationals Park in Washington, D.C., roughly two hours before a scheduled game between the Phillies and the Washington Nationals. He was rushed to George Washington University Hospital, where he was pronounced dead at the age of 73. The cause of death was attributed to heart disease.

The passing of Kalas evoked a profound outpouring of grief throughout Philadelphia and the sports world. On April 18, 2009, the Phillies hosted a public memorial service at Citizens Bank Park—the first time the ballpark was opened for a public funeral service. Tens of thousands of fans attended to pay their respects as Kalas's casket was placed at home plate.

The Phillies honored Kalas throughout the 2009 season by wearing a commemorative patch on their jerseys featuring his initials ("HK") over a black microphone. Several permanent honors were established in his memory:
* **The Harry Kalas Broadcast Booth:** The home radio broadcast booth at Citizens Bank Park was officially dedicated in his honor.
* **"Harry the K's":** A casual restaurant inside Citizens Bank Park located behind Section 141 was named after him.
* **Statue at Citizens Bank Park:** A 7.5-foot bronze statue of Kalas, sculpted by artist Lawrence Nowlan, was unveiled behind Section 141 in August 2011.
* **"High Hopes":** Following every Phillies home victory, the stadium sound system plays Kalas's recorded rendition of the Frank Sinatra song "High Hopes," a tradition that continues to this day.

Kalas's recognition in national halls of fame anchors his place in broadcasting history, highlighted by his 2002 Ford C. Frick Award from the National Baseball Hall of Fame and his 2008 induction into the National Sportscasters and Sportswriters Association Hall of Fame.
