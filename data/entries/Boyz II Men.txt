## 2. Lead section

Boyz II Men is an American R&B and vocal harmony group formed in Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, in 1988. Best known for emotional ballads and intricate four-part vocal harmonies, the group achieved international monumental stardom during the 1990s with Motown Records. Initially formed at the prestigious Philadelphia High School for the Creative and Performing Arts (CAPA), the group originally consisted of Nathan Morris, Wanya Morris, Shawn Stockman, Michael McCary, and Marc Nelson. Following Nelson's early departure before their debut album, the core quartet of Nathan Morris, Wanya Morris, Shawn Stockman, and Michael McCary spearheaded the group's global phenomenon.

During the 1990s, Boyz II Men redefined modern R&B by blending classic vocal harmony techniques rooted in Philadelphia soul and doo-wop with contemporary hip-hop soul and new jack swing production. Their groundbreaking single "End of the Road" (1992) topped the Billboard Hot 100 chart for a historic 13 weeks, breaking the decades-old record previously held by Elvis Presley. The group subsequently surpassed their own record with "I'll Make Love to You" (1994) and their iconic collaboration with Mariah Carey, "One Sweet Day" (1995), which set an unprecedented 16-week record at number one that remained unbroken for over two decades.

Beyond their chart dominance, Boyz II Men became one of the best-selling music groups in history, accumulating over 60 million records sold worldwide. Their multi-platinum albums, including *Cooleyhighharmony* (1991) and *II* (1994), solidified their legacy as cultural icons of 1990s popular music. Recognized with four Grammy Awards, nine American Music Awards, and a dedicated star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, the group occupies an essential place in the musical history of Philadelphia.

Despite transitioning into a trio in 2003 following Michael McCary's departure due to health complications related to scoliosis, Boyz II Men has continued to record, tour internationally, and perform extended residencies in Las Vegas, maintaining a continuous career spanning over three decades.

## 3. Early life and education

The origin of Boyz II Men is deeply intertwined with the educational and cultural ecosystem of Philadelphia in the late 1980s. The founding members—Nathan Morris, Marc Nelson, Wanya Morris, Shawn Stockman, and Michael McCary—were students at the Philadelphia High School for the Creative and Performing Arts (CAPA), a public magnet school located in South Philadelphia known for fostering exceptional musical and artistic talent.

Nathan Morris and Marc Nelson originally established an early iteration of the vocal group in 1988 under the name Unique Attraction. Operating within CAPA's rigorous music curriculum, the founding members honed their skills in vocal arrangement, classical choir performance, and music theory. Recognizing the exceptional acoustic properties of the school’s high-ceilinged restrooms, the young vocalists spent hours rehearsing vocal blends and acapella arrangements between classes and after school, taking inspiration from classic Philadelphia soul ensembles such as The Stylistics, The Delfonics, and Harold Melvin & the Blue Notes.

As new members joined—Wanya Morris, Shawn Stockman, and bass vocalist Michael McCary—the group refined its identity. Inspired by the vocal arrangements of the Boston-based R&B group New Edition, the ensemble renamed themselves "Boyz II Men," taking their title directly from New Edition's 1988 song "Boys to Men." Marc Nelson departed the ensemble shortly thereafter to pursue solo ventures and other artistic opportunities, solidifying the quartet lineup that would achieve worldwide acclaim.

The group's pivotal breakthrough occurred in 1989 at the Philadelphia Civic Center during a concert featuring New Edition's spin-off group, Bell Biv DeVoe. Boyz II Men sneaked backstage and performed an impassioned acapella rendition of New Edition's "Can You Stand the Rain" for group leader Michael Bivins. Impressed by their vocal precision, timing, and polished stage presence, Bivins agreed to manage the group and help secure a recording agreement with Motown Records, launching their professional recording career.

## 4. Career

Boyz II Men released their debut studio album, *Cooleyhighharmony*, on Motown Records in April 1991. Produced under the direction of Michael Bivins, Dallas Austin, L.A. Reid, and Babyface, the album was a critical and commercial triumph, debuting the group's signature "New Jack Doo-Wop" style—a fusion of urban new jack swing rhythms and smooth vocal harmonies. Driven by hit singles such as "Motownphilly" and "It's So Hard to Say Goodbye to Yesterday," *Cooleyhighharmony* sold over nine million copies in the United States alone and earned the group their first Grammy Award for Best R&B Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocals.

In 1992, the group contributed "End of the Road" to the soundtrack of the Eddie Murphy film *Boomerang*. Co-written and produced by L.A. Reid and Babyface, "End of the Road" became a monumental global pop hit. The track spent 13 consecutive weeks at number one on the Billboard Hot 100, establishing a new record for longevity at the top of the charts.

The group reached the zenith of their commercial power with their second studio album, *II*, released in 1994. Featuring production by Babyface and Jimmy Jam & Terry Lewis, *II* generated two back-to-back mega-hits: "I'll Make Love to You" and "On Bended Knee." "I'll Make Love to You" spent 14 weeks at number one, matching the record held by Whitney Houston's "I Will Always Love You," before being replaced at the top spot by "On Bended Knee." This made Boyz II Men only the third act in Billboard history—after The Beatles and Elvis Presley—to replace themselves at number one on the Hot 100. *II* was certified Diamond by the RIAA, selling over 12 million copies domestically.

In late 1995, Boyz II Men teamed up with pop icon Mariah Carey to record "One Sweet Day." The poignant track debuted at number one and held the position for 16 consecutive weeks, establishing an extraordinary record that stood for 23 years. The group followed this success with their 1997 album *Evolution*, which spawned another hit, "A Song for Mama," written for the feature film *Soul Food*.

During the early 2000s, the group navigated industry shifts and personal challenges. Bass singer Michael McCary left the group in 2003 due to chronic spinal problems caused by scoliosis, which limited his mobility. Continuing as a trio composed of Nathan Morris, Wanya Morris, and Shawn Stockman, Boyz II Men released several acclaimed studio albums, including *Full Circle* (2002), *Throwback, Vol. 1* (2004), *The Remedy* (2007), and *Twenty* (2011), celebrating their 20th anniversary in the music industry. In 2013, the group initiated a long-running, highly successful residency at The Mirage in Las Vegas, showcasing their enduring appeal to live audiences worldwide.

## 5. Personal life

The members of Boyz II Men have maintained distinct personal lives and philanthropic interests while remaining deeply connected to their Philadelphia roots.

* **Nathan Morris** (born June 18, 1971, in Philadelphia): Serves as the primary baritone singer and business manager of the group. Beyond music, Nathan has pursued real estate renovation and commercial investments, starring in the DIY Network television series *Hit Properties with Nathan Morris* and renovating historic properties. He has remained active in Philadelphia community development initiatives.
* **Wanya Morris** (born July 29, 1973, in Philadelphia): Known for his expressive tenor vocals and vocal range. Wanya married Traci Nash in 2002, and together they have raised a family with several children who have also pursued careers in music. In 2016, Wanya competed on Season 22 of ABC’s *Dancing with the Stars*, reaching the semifinals.
* **Shawn Stockman** (born November 29, 1972, in Philadelphia): Serves as a tenor vocalist, songwriter, and musician. Stockman married Sharonda Jones in 2001. After one of his twin sons was diagnosed with autism, Stockman established the non-profit organization **Micah's Voice**, which provides financial assistance, support, and resources to families caring for children on the autism spectrum. Stockman has also served as a judge on the NBC acapella competition show *The Sing-Off*.
* **Michael McCary** (born December 16, 1971, in Philadelphia): Former bass vocalist whose deep tone defined the group's spoken-word interludes and low harmonies. Following his retirement from the group in 2003, McCary focused on managing his health and managing chronic spinal difficulties associated with scoliosis.

## 6. Legacy

Boyz II Men's legacy within popular culture, R&B, and Philadelphia musical history is profound. They are widely acknowledged as the premier vocal group of the 1990s, responsible for revitalizing vocal harmony ensembles in mainstream popular music. Their incredible chart dominance paved the way for subsequent boy bands, R&B groups, and vocal pop ensembles throughout the late 1990s and 2000s, including 98 Degrees, Jagged Edge, 112, and NSYNC.

The group's technical proficiency—characterized by effortless lead trades, complex polyphonic vocal arrangements, and smooth pitch control—elevated the standard of contemporary vocal performance. Music historians frequently cite Boyz II Men alongside historic Motown acts like The Temptations and Philadelphia soul legends like The O'Jays as custodians of American vocal group tradition.

In recognition of their cultural contributions, the City of Philadelphia officially designated a portion of Broad Street near their alma mater CAPA as **"Boyz II Men Boulevard"** in 2017. The group was inducted into the Vocal Group Hall of Fame in 2011 and awarded a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame at 7060 Hollywood Boulevard in 2012. Their enduring impact continues to be celebrated across generations of musicians, recording artists, and soul enthusiasts globally.
