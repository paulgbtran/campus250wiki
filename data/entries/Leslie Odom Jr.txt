### Lead section

**Leslie Odom Jr.** (born August 6, 1981) is an American actor, vocalist, and author whose artistic trajectory highlights the connection between Philadelphia’s public arts education ecosystem and contemporary American theater and film. Widely recognized for originating the role of Aaron Burr in the Broadway musical *Hamilton*, Odom’s performance earned him the 2016 Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical and a Grammy Award. His career spans Broadway stages, major film productions, solo recording projects, and published literature, positioning him as a major figure in 21st-century American performing arts.

Raised in the East Mount Airy neighborhood of Philadelphia, Odom developed his early performance skills within local civic and educational institutions. His formative experiences at Philadelphia’s historic Freedom Theatre, the Julia R. Masterman School, and the Philadelphia High School for Creative and Performing Arts (CAPA) provided the foundation for his professional debut on Broadway at the age of seventeen in *Rent*. This early background in the Delaware Valley’s cultural network served as a launching pad for his higher education at Carnegie Mellon University and his subsequent career across television, theater, and cinema.

Following his success in *Hamilton*, Odom expanded his creative output into national media, film, and literary authorship. In March 2018, he published his inspirational memoir and self-development book, *Failing Up: How to Take Risks, Aim Higher, and Never Stop Learning*, which he promoted during appearances on regional public media outlets including WHYY’s flagship call-in program *Radio Times*. His film work, notably his portrayal of legendary soul singer Sam Cooke in *One Night in Miami...* (2020), further cemented his national standing, earning him Academy Award nominations for both acting and original song composition.

Within the broader context of Philadelphia history, Odom represents a prominent modern successor to the city’s rich legacy of African American performing artists. His ongoing engagement with the region’s cultural institutions, public broadcasting media, and youth educational initiatives illustrates the continuous influence of Philadelphia’s arts training programs on the global stage.

### Early life and education

Leslie Odom Jr. was born in Queens, New York, but was raised primarily in the East Mount Airy neighborhood of Northwest Philadelphia. Growing up in a middle-class African American family in Philadelphia during the late 1980s and 1990s, Odom was immersed in a community that prioritized educational achievement and artistic expression. His early interest in speech, theater, and vocal performance was cultivated within regional institutions that historically served as incubators for local creative talent.

Odom attended the Julia R. Masterman Laboratory and Demonstration School for middle school before attending the Philadelphia High School for Creative and Performing Arts (CAPA). Masterman’s rigorous academic environment and CAPA's focused arts curriculum were complemented by Odom's involvement in regional oratorical contests and local theater programs. During his youth, Odom undertook formative dramatic training at the Freedom Theatre, located on North Broad Street. As Pennsylvania’s oldest African American theater company, Freedom Theatre played a pivotal role in shaping Odom’s artistic discipline, offering a supportive environment focused on performance technique, cultural heritage, and community empowerment.

Recognizing his potential, Odom’s instructors encouraged him to pursue professional opportunities early. At seventeen, while still completing his high school studies in Philadelphia, Odom auditioned for and was cast in the Broadway production of Jonathan Larson's rock musical *Rent*, replacing an ensemble member and understudying the role of Benjamin Coffin III (Benny). Following his high school graduation, Odom pursued higher education at Carnegie Mellon University’s School of Drama in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, graduating with a Bachelor of Fine Arts in 2003.

---

### Career

Odom’s early post-collegiate career involved a mix of episodic television appearances, regional theater, and recurring roles on network television series such as *CSI: Miami*, *Vanished*, and *Smash*. However, his career reached a historic peak when he collaborated with playwright and composer Lin-Manuel Miranda on the off-Broadway and subsequent Broadway production of *Hamilton* (2015–2016). 

Portraying Aaron Burr, the complex antagonist and narrator of the musical, Odom received widespread critical acclaim. His performance, highlighted by show-stopping numbers such as "The Room Where It Happens" and "Wait for It," earned him the 2016 Tony Award for Best Actor in a Musical, beating out co-star Miranda, as well as a Grammy Award as a principal soloist on the original cast recording.

Following his departure from *Hamilton* in July 2016, Odom embarked on a multi-faceted career encompassing solo recording, national concerts, feature films, and writing:
* **Literary Contributions:** In March 2018, Odom released his book *Failing Up: How to Take Risks, Aim Higher, and Never Stop Learning*. Designed as an extended commencement address, the book details his personal journey from Philadelphia to Broadway, emphasizing resilience, personal accountability, and the value of incremental growth. On March 28, 2018, Odom appeared on WHYY’s public radio program *Radio Times* with host Marty Moss-Coane to discuss the memoir, his connection to Philadelphia, and his artistic philosophy.
* **Film and Screen:** Odom starred in major cinematic projects, including the 2017 feature film adaptation of *Murder on the Orient Express* and Regina King’s 2020 historical drama *One Night in Miami...*. His performance as soul icon Sam Cooke in the latter garnered Academy Award nominations for Best Supporting Actor and Best Original Song (for writing and performing "Speak Now").
* **Music:** As a recording artist, Odom has released multiple studio albums featuring jazz, pop, and holiday repertoire, touring extensively with national symphony orchestras and solo backing ensembles.

### Personal life

Leslie Odom Jr. married actress and singer Nicolette Robinson in December 2012, after meeting her in 2008 during the audition process for the Los Angeles production of the musical *Once on This Island*. The couple has two children, a daughter born in 2017 and a son born in 2021.

Odom maintains strong personal and professional connections to the Philadelphia metropolitan area. Throughout his career, he has publicly credited his upbringing in East Mount Airy, his middle-class family upbringing, and the mentorship of local Philadelphia educators for instilling the discipline required for long-term survival in the arts. His personal philosophy—centered on personal growth through overcoming setback, as outlined in his 2018 book *Failing Up*—frequently draws directly on his childhood experiences in the Delaware Valley.

---

### Legacy

Leslie Odom Jr.'s legacy in Philadelphia history lies at the intersection of public education, community cultural institutions, and national artistic achievement. His rise from local youth theater programs to global theatrical prominence serves as a model for the efficacy of regional arts investment. By frequently citing institutions like the Freedom Theatre and public magnet schools like Masterman and CAPA as key drivers of his success, Odom reinforces the role of Philadelphia's civic infrastructure in nurturing artistic talent.

Furthermore, Odom's post-*Hamilton* work—spanning book publishing, public broadcast appearances on networks like WHYY, and film production—highlights his influence beyond musical theater. His work as an author and public speaker has made him a prominent voice in contemporary conversations on mentorship, creative risk-taking, and career development for young artists throughout the Delaware Valley and across the country.
