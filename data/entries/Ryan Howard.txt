## Lead section

Ryan Alexander Howard (born November 19, 1979) is an American former professional baseball first baseman who played his entire 13-year Major League Baseball (MLB) career with the Philadelphia Phillies from 2004 through 2016. Nicknamed "The Big Piece," Howard was one of the most feared left-handed power hitters of his generation and served as the offensive cornerstone during the most successful era in Philadelphia Phillies franchise history.

Howard achieved immediate stardom upon entering the major leagues. After winning the National League (NL) Rookie of the Year Award in 2005, he assembled one of the most powerful offensive campaigns in baseball history in 2006, winning the NL Most Valuable Player (MVP) Award after setting the Phillies' single-season record with 58 home runs and driving in 149 runs. Howard became the fastest player in MLB history to reach both 100 and 200 career home runs, cementing his reputation as a generational run-producer.

As the central slugger in a lineup featuring Jimmy Rollins, Chase Utley, and Shane Victorino, Howard led the Phillies to five consecutive National League East division titles from 2007 to 2011, back-to-back National League pennants in 2008 and 2009, and the franchise’s second World Series championship in 2008. His postseason prowess, which included winning the 2009 NLCS MVP and hitting two home runs in Game 4 of the 2008 World Series, established him as a legendary figure in Philadelphia sports history.

Following his retirement from active play, Howard was widely celebrated for his contributions to the organization and the city of Philadelphia. In 2024, he was officially enshrined into the Philadelphia Phillies Wall of Fame at Citizens Bank Park, securing his permanent legacy among the greatest historical figures in Philadelphia sports.

## Early life and education

Ryan Howard was born on November 19, 1979, in St. Louis, Missouri, to Ron and Cheryl Howard. Raised alongside his twin brother, Corey, in a family that emphasized both academics and athletics, Howard developed an early passion for sports. He played brass instruments in his school marching band while simultaneously honing his skills on the baseball diamond and the basketball court.

Howard attended Lafayette High School in Wildwood, Missouri, where he distinguished himself as a standout multi-sport athlete. On the baseball field, his imposing physical stature and natural power swing quickly drew the attention of local scouts. Upon graduating from high school, Howard enrolled at Missouri State University (then known as Southwest Missouri State University) in Springfield, Missouri, to play collegiate baseball for the Bears.

During his collegiate career from 1999 to 2001, Howard established himself as a premier collegiate power hitter. In his freshman season in 1999, he batted .355 with 19 home runs and 66 RBIs in 58 games, earning Missouri Valley Conference (MVC) Freshman of the Year honors. Across three seasons at SMSU, Howard posted a career .335 batting average with 50 home runs and 183 RBIs. His collegiate performance solidified his status as a top draft prospect, leading the Philadelphia Phillies to select him in the fifth round (140th overall) of the 2001 Major League Baseball Draft.

## Career

### Minor League Ascent and Rookie of the Year (2001–2005)

After signing with the Phillies, Howard progressed rapidly through the club's farm system, playing for minor league affiliates including the Batavia Muckdogs, Lakewood BlueClaws, Clearwater Threshers, and Reading Phillies. In 2004, Howard hit 37 home runs for Double-A Reading (and 46 home runs overall between Double-A and Triple-A Scranton/Wilkes-Barre), earning the Eastern League MVP Award. Despite his minor league dominance, his path to the majors was initially blocked by Philadelphia's veteran All-Star first baseman, Jim Thome. 

Howard made his MLB debut on September 1, 2004, appearing as a late-season call-up and hitting his first major league home run on September 11 against the New York Mets. Prior to the 2005 season, trade rumors swirled around Howard due to Thome's presence. However, when Thome suffered an elbow injury in June 2005, Howard was recalled to take over everyday duties at first base. In just 88 games, Howard launched 22 home runs, recorded 63 RBIs, and posted a .288 batting average. His midseason surge propelled the Phillies' wild-card chase and earned him the 2005 NL Rookie of the Year Award, prompting the franchise to trade Thome to the Chicago White Sox in the offseason to clear first base permanently for Howard.

### MVP Season and World Series Champion (2006–2009)

The 2006 season marked one of the most dominant offensive years by a hitter in modern baseball history. Howard won the 2006 Home Run Derby at PNC Park in Pittsburgh during All-Star weekend, setting the stage for an extraordinary second half. He finished the season with a .313 batting average, a franchise-record 58 home runs, and a league-leading 149 RBIs. Howard broke Mike Schmidt's single-season team record for home runs (48) and became the first Phillie to win the National League MVP Award since Schmidt in 1986.

Howard continued his historical pace over the next three seasons. In 2007, despite missing time with a quadriceps injury, he hit 47 home runs and drove in 136 runs, becoming the fastest player in MLB history to reach 100 home runs (accomplished in just 325 games). In 2008, Howard played in all 162 regular-season games, leading the major leagues with 48 home runs and 146 RBIs while placing second in NL MVP voting. In the 2008 postseason, Howard hit three home runs overall, including two home runs during Game 4 of the World Series against the Tampa Bay Rays, helping Philadelphia capture its first major sports championship in 25 years.

In 2009, Howard again paced the National League with 141 RBIs while hitting 45 home runs. During the 2009 National League Championship Series (NLCS) against the Los Angeles Dodgers, Howard batted .333 with two home runs and eight RBIs in five games, earning NLCS MVP honors as the Phillies returned to the World Series.

### Later Career, Achilles Injury, and Retirement (2010–2018)

In April 2010, Howard signed a five-year, $125 million contract extension with the Phillies. He continued to produce heavy RBI totals, driving in 108 runs in 2010 and 116 runs in 2011 while leading Philadelphia to a franchise-record 102 wins in 2011. However, the trajectory of Howard's career was drastically altered on the final play of Game 5 of the 2011 National League Division Series (NLDS) against the St. Louis Cardinals. Grounding out to end the game and the Phillies' season, Howard ruptured his left Achilles tendon while stepping off the batter's box.

The devastating injury required extensive surgery and sidelined Howard for the first half of the 2012 season. Although he returned to field duties, the lingering physical effects affected his agility and power generation. Despite struggling with subsequent injuries, Howard managed to surpass 300 career home runs in 2014 and recorded 23 home runs with 95 RBIs in 2014, followed by 25 home runs in his final season with Philadelphia in 2016. 

Howard played his final game as a Phillie on October 2, 2016, receiving an emotional standing ovation from the crowd at Citizens Bank Park. He subsequently signed minor league contracts with the Atlanta Braves and Colorado Rockies organizations in 2017 before officially announcing his retirement from professional baseball in September 2018.

## Personal life

Off the field, Howard is actively involved in business enterprises, broadcast media, and philanthropic efforts. In 2012, he married Krystal Buscha, a former elementary school teacher and cheerleader. The couple has four children and resides primarily in the Philadelphia area and Florida.

Howard has demonstrated a long-standing commitment to youth empowerment and literacy. Alongside his wife, he co-authored a popular children’s book series titled *Little Rhino*, published by Scholastic Inc., which aims to teach young readers lessons about teamwork, perseverance, and sportsmanship. Through the Ryan Howard Family Foundation, he has donated millions of dollars, equipment, and resources to underrepresented youth programs, public schools, and baseball organizations in both St. Louis and the Greater Philadelphia region.

In his post-playing career, Howard transitioned into venture capital as a partner at SeventySix Capital, a Philadelphia-based venture capital firm investing in sports technology, esports, and health companies. He has also served as an analyst for ESPN and Major League Baseball international broadcasts.

## Legacy

Ryan Howard remains one of the most revered icons in Philadelphia sports history. Known affectionately to fans and teammates as "The Big Piece"—a moniker bestowed by legendary manager Charlie Manuel to highlight Howard's indispensability to the roster—he came to symbolize the relentless, high-powered offensive identity of the 2007–2011 Phillies dynasty.

Howard's career record for home run acceleration stands among the most remarkable in baseball lore; he holds the MLB records for being the fastest player to hit 100 home runs (325 games) and 200 home runs (658 games). He finished his major league career with 382 home runs and 1,194 RBIs, placing him second in Phillies history in career home runs behind only Hall of Famer Mike Schmidt.

Howard's career achievements were permanently codified by the organization on August 18, 2024, when he was officially inducted into the Philadelphia Phillies Wall of Fame during a pregame ceremony at Citizens Bank Park. Surrounded by former teammates, franchise legends, and thousands of fans, Howard's bronze plaque was unveiled in Ashburn Alley, guaranteeing that his impact on the franchise and the city of Philadelphia will be celebrated for generations to come.
